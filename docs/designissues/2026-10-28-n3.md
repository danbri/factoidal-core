## 1. Targets and versions

`third_party/testing/n3`; versions and files in

## 2. How N3 relates to Turtle (RDF 1.1 Turtle and RDF 1.2 Turtle)

Notation3 Language § "Relationship to Other Languages": "N3 is a superset of
Turtle". The N3 grammar is the Turtle grammar with these changes:

| N3 production | Change from Turtle |
|---|---|
| [9] `triples ::= subject predicateObjectList?` | a subject may stand alone |
| [12] `verb` | adds `has e`, `is e of`, `=` (`owl:sameAs`), `=>` (`log:implies`), `<=` (`log:isImpliedBy`) |
| [14] `predicate ::= expression \| '<-' expression` | any term as predicate; `<-` inverts |
| [17] `path ::= pathItem ('!' path \| '^' path)?` | paths |
| [18] `pathItem` | quick variables `?x`, graph terms `{ }`, literals and lists in any position |
| [21] `iriPropertyList` | `[ id <iri> ... ]` |
| [23], [24] `formula`, `formulaContent` | statements and directives inside `{ }`, the last `.` optional |

Every terminal is a Turtle terminal. So the Lean reader
(`formal/lean4/L4Factoidal/N3/Parser.lean`) calls the Turtle reader's own
functions for all of them (`Syntax/Turtle.lean`: `tws`, `readTurtleIri`,
`readTurtleBnode`, `readRdfLiteral`, `readAnyLiteral`, `readPnameNs`,
`readIriRef`, `matchKeyword`, `TurtleState`), and writes only the N3
productions above them: one `mutual` block of nine functions. Rule 1 of the
issue comment (reuse first): there is no second IRI, string, number or prefix
reader.

The abstract syntax (`N3/Syntax.lean`, `N3Term`) is Notation3 Semantics §
"Abstract syntax": IRIs and literals are the RDF terms of `RDF/Core.lean`
(`WfIri`, `WfLiteral`), plus blank nodes, quick variables, lists as terms, and
graph terms. A graph term's blank nodes are scoped to that graph term (the
community-group consensus the Semantics draft states); the reader gives each
graph term's `_:x` a label unique to that graph term, so the quantification
sets are computable from the labels.

`N3/Rdf.lean` converts an N3 graph that is an RDF graph to RDF triples, with
lists as `rdf:first`/`rdf:rest` collections (RDF 1.1 Turtle § 2.8), which is the
reading the Turtle reader gives the same text. That is the relation the theorem
of § 5 states.

### RDF 1.2 Turtle

The N3 drafts say nothing about RDF 1.2 ("RDF-star: TODO" in the Language
draft). In RDF 1.2 mode (`Mode.rdf12`) the reader also reads the RDF 1.2 Turtle
productions, each with the Turtle reader's own function: [30] `tripleTerm`
(`readTripleTerm`), [27] `reifiedTriple` (`readReifiedTriple`), [33] `reifier`
and [34] `annotation` (`readAnnotations`), `VERSION`, and directional language
tags. A triple term is the `N3Term.triple` term, which holds the RDF 1.2 triple
term of `RDF/Core.lean`. So in RDF 1.2 mode N3 stays a superset of RDF 1.2
Turtle (decision 3).

## 3. Decisions

1. **Grammar over prose where the Language draft contradicts itself.** The
   draft's § "EBNF Grammar" says "Supporting all verb and boolean keywords ...
   both with and without '@' prefix"; § "Changes since the Team Submission"
   says "Removed `@a`, `@is`, `@of`, and `@has` in favor of `a`, `is`, `of` and
   `has`", and the grammar has no `@` keywords. The reader follows the grammar
   and the Changes section: no `@a`, `@is`, `@of`, `@has`, `@true`, `@false`,
   `@keywords`, `@forAll`, `@forSome`. The suite marks the tests that use them
   `rdft:Rejected`; they run and are counted apart.
2. **The empty prefix `:` is bound to `<#>` until the document binds it.**
   No text of the drafts says what an undeclared `:` means; the Turtle grammar
   makes it an error. A text scan of the parser manifest finds 35 action files
   of tests that are not `rdft:Rejected` that use `:` with no declaration;
   the eval results expect `<#>` (the retrieval IRI with the fragment `#`,
   for example `<https://w3c.github.io/N3/tests/N3Tests/cwm_syntax/numbers.n3#is>`),
   the convention of cwm and the Team Submission. The N3 reader binds it
   (`parseN3`); the Turtle reader does not change. So two Turtle negative tests
   (`turtle-syntax-bad-prefix-01`, `-02`) are N3 documents
   (`n3ValidTurtleNegative` in `Harness/N3Run.lean`).
3. **RDF 1.2 Turtle forms in RDF 1.2 mode** (§ 2). Closer to the RDF 1.2
   direction, the owner's rule of 2026-10-27 for JSON-LD-star ("Go for whatever
   is closer to RDF 1.2 direction"), applied here by the session. A triple
   term may stand in any position, as any N3 term may, so four RDF 1.2 Turtle
   negative tests (triple term or reified triple as subject or predicate) are
   N3 documents. An annotation `{| |}` or reifier `~` is allowed only after an
   object whose asserted triple is an RDF triple; its block is read by the
   Turtle production, so it holds RDF terms only.
4. **Paths left to right.** [17] is written right-recursive, but a path is a
   sequence of steps from a resource (Language § "SPARQL": "It is assumed that
   the path starts from a resource"). `:a!:b^:c` is `(:a!:b)^:c`: each step
   makes a fresh blank node.
5. **Nesting bound.** At most `maxNesting` = 2048 levels of `{ }`, `( )`,
   `[ ]`. The suite's `cwm_syntax/too-nested.n3` (`rdft:Proposed`) has about
   1,100 levels and reads. A level costs four native frames; the wasm stack is
   8 MB.
6. **`{}` and `true`.** Notation3 Semantics § "Log Semantics": "The literal
   `true` stands for the graph term <∅, ∅, ∅>". The reader keeps what was
   written; the comparison (`N3/Compare.lean`) reads `{}` as `true`, as the
   suite's `empty_graph` tests expect.
7. **Turtle negative tests that are N3.** Nineteen negative tests of the
   suite's own `TurtleTests` (and the same names in the RDF 1.1 Turtle suite)
   are valid N3 (literal subjects and predicates, graph terms, `=`, `=>`,
   paths, ...). `Harness/N3Run.lean` lists each with the N3 production that
   admits it, and the N3 reader must read it.

## 4. N3 rules, SPARQL-RL and RIF Core (steps 2 to 4 of the tracker)

An N3 rule is a triple `{ body } => { head }` (`log:implies`); `<=` is the same
rule written backwards. Notation3 Semantics § "Log Semantics" gives
`log:implies` its meaning: for each assignment of the universal variables that
makes the body true, the head is true.

**The Datalog fragment.** A rule whose body and head are graph terms of triple
patterns over IRIs, literals and quick variables, with no nested graph term,
no list, no built-in predicate in the body, every head variable in the body,
and no blank node in the head. Its meaning is the Datalog rule; the closure of
a graph under a set of such rules is the least fixpoint of the immediate
consequence operator.

**Reuse.** That fragment is the fragment of SPARQL-RL rules with only
triple-pattern body elements and no `NOT`, no `SET`, no blank-node head
(`SPARQLRL/Syntax.lean`, `Rule`). So the reasoner translates the N3 rule set to
a SPARQL-RL `RuleSet` and runs the SPARQL-RL evaluator
(`SPARQLRL/Eval.lean`: stratification, semi-naive rounds, an indexed graph),
whose semi-naive fixpoint is proved equal to the naive one
(`SPARQLRL/SemiNaiveTheorems.lean`). RIF Core (`RIF/Engine.lean`) has the same
Horn fragment; a later theorem can relate the three (N3 Datalog fragment, the
SPARQL-RL rules, RIF Core rules) once two of them are proved equal.

**Theorem 4b.** For a rule set in the Datalog fragment, the closure that
`N3.Reason` defines directly (the least fixpoint of the N3 rules over the data
triples) equals the inference graph of `SPARQLRL.infer` on the translation,
plus the data. Version-named: Notation3 Semantics at the pinned commit, and the
SPARQL 1.2 RL editor's draft at commit `fe4eed4`
(`third_party/testing/sparql-rl-PROVENANCE.md`).

**Beyond the fragment.** Blank nodes in a head (existential rules), nested
graph terms, lists, and built-ins. Built-ins come in groups (`math:`,
`string:`, `list:`, `log:`), each with its Builtins-draft version; where a
built-in's semantics is a SPARQL 1.2 function's (`string:concatenation` and
`CONCAT`, `math:sum` and `+` on numbers, `string:matches` and `REGEX`), the
reasoner evaluates it with `SPARQL.Expr.evalIn` and records the agreement as a
theorem or a differential test. A built-in whose semantics differs
(`math:sum` over a list, `string:lessThan` on code points) gets its own Lean
definition.

## 7. Costs paid and open

- The comparison of the eval tests (`N3/Compare.lean`) is individualisation and
  refinement over 64-bit hashes: a test oracle, not a specification (its module
  header states the collision case). The first version re-scanned every triple
  for every blank node in each round; files of the extended suite with 2,000
  blank nodes took more than 10 minutes. With an index of the triples by label
  and the round trip checked by first-occurrence relabelling, the extended
  suite runs in 5 seconds.
- No npm or wasm entry point yet (step 5 of the tracker).
