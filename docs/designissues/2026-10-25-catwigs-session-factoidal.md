# Catwigs session: Factoidal as a whole

Status: suspended by the owner on 2026-10-26 ("Suspend catwigs conversation;
but save"), after the seven cards and before the closing decision.
Date: 2026-10-25 and 2026-10-26. Mode: guided conversation, one participant
(the owner), facilitated by Claude.

Method: Catwigs, by Libby Miller and Richard Sewell
(https://github.com/libbymiller/catwigs_cards, https://catwigs.org/), used
through a text adaptation of the cards, version 1.0.0, licensed CC BY-NC 4.0
(https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/). The card questions and
example labels below are quoted from that adaptation. The placements are
comparisons, not scores; they are not summed or averaged.

Selection: seven cards chosen by the facilitator for a varied discussion; not
a random draw: `01_problem`, `04_clear`, `03_discoverable`, `05_enjoyable`,
`16_difficult`, `20_interests`, `22_fail`.

Basis of every placement: the owner's report in the session. No other
participant took part, so no card is marked contentious.

## Cards

### 01_problem — Does it solve a problem? (Cat wig; Garage door remote; Antibiotics)

Placement: Antibiotics. The owner named five problems, owner's words:

1. "an unsually self-contained, coherent, cross-platform integrative and
   complete implementation of W3C's longstanding activities around web
   standards for data, rdf, ontologies, markup etc";
2. "positive moves on the questions around how rdf, opendata, open standards
   and open source communities respond to the challenges and opportunities of
   modern ai";
3. "help W3C groups have full working implementations from d[a]y 1, and
   suggest templates for the groups to characterise their specs in ways that
   are formal yet immedia[tely] practically useful";
4. "helps anyone building with AI to move beyond chaotic 'vibe' efforts and
   have confidence in the properties of their work";
5. "open up contributions to thousands of ai-boosted collaborators online ...
   We could accept code from strangers, by exploiting assurances baked into
   the project structure, CI, tests and definitions."

Thresholds, owner, verbatim: "Worthwhile: problem 1; extraordinary: 2 or more
crom [from] 3,4,5. Item 2 is vague, but roughly most responses so far are
defensive or trivial."

Facilitator note: the five problems have different holders; Garage door remote
can fit some holders (for example an RDF developer who already has an engine).

### 04_clear — Is it clear what it is? (Conceptual art; Connected TV; A Spoon)

Placement: two positions, not a midpoint. Owner: "A twin peaks distribution
here maybe - conceptual art but also tediously practical." Conceptual art for
the larger framing (formal grounding, method for AI, contribution model); A
Spoon for the practical engine.

On whether the split needs repair, owner: "It's worth considering in
presentation materials, documentation etx". No action agreed.

### 03_discoverable — Is it discoverable? (Four leaf clover; TV instruction manual; Light switch)

Placement: TV instruction manual (the owner wrote "tv remote", read as this
middle example), heading towards Light switch. Owner: "It is very early and
easy to over-promise. Fixed some important stabilisation and credibility
issues this week, and cranking up who gets to see it."

### 05_enjoyable — Is it enjoyable? (Tax return; Sitcom; Fireworks)

Placement: Fireworks, for the owner as the person directing the work
(confirmed: "Oh Fireworks"). Reason, owner: "I can get a lot done quickly in
general with ai coding, and the combination of using a formal language as
tool, a formal language as target, and the support and ecosystem of w3c (test
suites, interrelated specs) is intriguing and underexplored."

For developers who use it, an expectation, not yet evidence: "If it simply
does what the specs say and autodocuments any shortfalls it should be a
pleasing dx too."

### 16_difficult — Is it difficult to build? (A house; A shed; A tent)

Placement: A house, the hardest end. Owner: "House! Methods that don't exist
yet".

### 20_interests — Is it in our best interests? (No!; Yes; Enables growth)

Placement: positive; no example named. Owner: "Pretty positive. Getting best
from a controversial technology."

Open: which "we" (the owner, the RDF and W3C communities, contributors).

### 22_fail — What happens if we fail? (Ignominy; Sadness; Quietly forgotten)

Placement: Quietly forgotten. Owner: "Quietly forgot sounds about right. Many
things noisy right now."

Facilitator note: quiet failure is not harmless for people who rely on the
project's guarantees; for them a wrong guarantee is closer to Sadness, and the
risk grows with the audience.

## Proposed priorities (facilitator's proposal, not agreed)

1. Difficulty against the worthwhile threshold (cards 16 and 01): the methods
   do not exist yet, so the method is the main risk.
2. Clarity and discovery (cards 04 and 03), as the audience is widened.
3. The open "we" (card 20) and the reliance risk (card 22), before strangers
   contribute or depend on the project.
